# Research and inspiration sources

Use these as starting points, not authorities. Always verify current information and distinguish evidence from inspiration.

## Product and industrial design
- Core77 — industrial design practice, process, criticism, portfolios, and industry discussion.
- Designboom — broad contemporary design and architecture reference.
- Car Design News — transportation and automotive design reference.
- Red Dot and iF award sites — organiser self-description of criteria and awarded work: a source for vocabulary and examples, not for quality claims. Quote the edition's wording and stamp the edition. Award schemes are jury judgments on paid, self-selected entries. Store no fees or weights.

## Digital product and interaction design
- Material Design by Google — interaction, accessibility, components, and platform patterns.
- Apple Human Interface Guidelines — platform behavior and interaction conventions.
- W3C/WAI — accessibility standards and guidance.
- Nielsen Norman Group — usability and UX research references.

## Research discipline

For every source, label the role it plays:

**Evidence** — data or documented behavior relevant to users or constraints.

**Reference** — an existing solution worth studying.

**Trend signal** — evidence of a growing aesthetic, behavior, technology, or market direction.

**Inspiration** — useful stimulus that should not be copied literally.

**Inference** — a reasoned conclusion by the agent that has not yet been validated.

A mood board full of attractive work is not user research.

## Internet research protocol

When web access is available:
1. Start from the brief and user questions, not broad browsing.
2. Search across direct competitors, adjacent categories, and outside-category inspiration.
3. Prefer primary sources for factual claims.
4. Record publication dates for time-sensitive material.
5. Capture contradictions, not just confirming evidence.
6. Stop when additional sources are repeating the same signal.
7. Turn research into design implications.

When web access is unavailable, state assumptions and create a research plan rather than fabricating findings.
